# T491 nominal reflow lands: two explicit local footprints

**2026-09-27 — design choice, not an assembly release.** The two unchanged
KEMET parts now use project-local footprints implementing Table 2, density B
(median/nominal), of T2005_T491 dated 2026-07-08. This resolves the earlier
unexplained generic-land discrepancy for C_REF and C_VCAP1. It does not resolve
Murata assembly evidence, the other generic footprints, or physical qualification.

## Decision and source

Use the manufacturer's nominal reflow land pattern as the design target for
these two tantalum packages, rather than retaining generic KiCad patterns that
were not equal to any of its A/B/C rows. Density B is a deliberate moderate-density
reflow design choice. It is not a claim that an assembler, paste/stencil or
actual board process has already been selected and qualified. Density A's wider
process window and density C's minimum pattern are not silently mixed into B.
Any future change of soldering/process strategy must revisit this decision.

The public manufacturer PDF was directly captured and visually checked, including
Table 2 and its diagram on printed page 15, and body dimensions on page 4.
The table distinguishes land length L, width W, inner gap S, and complete
courtyard dimensions V1/V2. These are different quantities; S is not pad-centre
spacing and a courtyard is not additional copper.

| Unchanged part / role | New footprint | L × W (mm) | Inner gap S | Full courtyard V1 × V2 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| T491B226K016AT / 22 uF reference | RevA_Passives:T491B_3528_DensityB | 1.80 × 2.23 | 1.12 mm | 5.22 × 3.50 mm |
| T491D107K016AT / 100 uF VCAP1 | RevA_Passives:T491D_7343_DensityB | 2.37 × 2.43 | 3.87 mm | 9.12 × 5.10 mm |

Pad centres are at X = ±(S+L)/2: ±1.46 mm for case B and ±3.12 mm for D.
Rectangular copper implements the table's rectangular lands; no rounded-corner
area correction or pad-level mask/paste override is assumed. The final board's
mask, stencil and solder-process settings still need review. Fab outlines use
nominal bodies; courtyard envelopes use the table's V1/V2, not the nominal body.
No possibly inaccurate 3D model is attached to these local files.

KEMET identifies the positive terminal by a stripe and sometimes a bevel. Here
pad 1 is positive on the local left, with positive Fab/Silk marks and a chamfer;
pad 2 is the return. The schematic still connects C_REF pad 1 to VREFP and
C_VCAP1 pad 1 to VCAP1, with both pad 2 terminals at ground. A PCB rotation must
preserve this pad/net association. Native exports were visually reviewed; no
physical component orientation or assembled board was inspected.

## What changes, and what does not

The editable `.kicad_mod` files live in
`hardware/rev_a/kicad/RevA_Passives.pretty`. The local library entry resolves only
`${KIPRJMOD}/RevA_Passives.pretty`. It never falls back to a similarly named
installed library. The existing source boundary requires exactly these two
local files, rejects missing/extra/link inputs, validates their geometry, and
hashes them even when no external footprint directory is supplied.

Seven selected footprint definitions remain in the pinned installed KiCad
libraries; two are now local. The total remains nine definitions, 102 unique
pads, 68 daughterboard footprints and 69 schematic components including MOD1.
A CAD-only snapshot grows from seven to nine files; the full schematic gate
still hashes 44 inputs because two footprint inputs moved from installed to
local storage rather than being added to the selected component set.

Only the C_REF and C_VCAP1 footprint IDs change in the fresh XML/CSV exports.
All 256 terminal net memberships, pin types/functions, component references,
values, MPNs, population and other component fields are unchanged. The BOM JSON,
board-profile JSON, firmware, rails and dependency lock are byte-unchanged.
This is not a replacement of either tantalum capacitor or of the obsolete MLCC.

## Checks and fixture provenance

Four initial tests failed before implementation: the two old selected footprint
IDs and the two absent local native files. Independent expected dimensions come
from the manufacturer's table, not the footprint under test. New regression
cases reject old generic lands, reversed/renumbered terminals, undersized or
missing courtyards, copper graphics, undeclared files, symlinks and an external
library fallback. The existing pad fault sweep and nonpolar controls remain.

The current geometry checker checks the local courtyard as one unfilled front
rectangle in addition to copper; other libraries retain their previous graphics
scope. Numeric tolerance remains a representation tolerance, not fabrication
clearance. Silkscreen legibility, final-board overlap, mask/stencil, actual mating
and assembly yield are not established by these source checks.

The frozen XML and CSV are newly generated by actual KiCad 9.0.2 from this CAD;
they are not hand-edited expected outputs. Their parser-level comparison against
the previous fixture permits exactly the two declared footprint substitutions
and proves all remaining graph/part fields unchanged. The native suite exports
all nine currently selected footprints and regenerates the actual schematic.
The two frozen local footprint inputs are project-authored MIT files; the seven
unmodified installed-library fixtures retain the KiCad library license.

Reproduce with the existing entry point:

```sh
uv run --locked python -m tools.check --native --schematic
```

No generator, new dependency, second BOM or new verification runner is needed.
The ordinary and native tests remain distinct from the hosted target compiler
and from physical hardware evidence. All release/purchase/body-use gates stay
false; actual soldering or fabrication is not authorized by this document.

## Independent review correction: courtyard stroke

Codex independently found that endpoints and fill alone did not bind the
rendered courtyard: a 10 mm stroke could replace 0.05 mm without rejection.
Twelve altered, absent, duplicated, wrong-style and extra-field controls failed
before correction on the two local patterns; two harmless numeric/order controls
passed. The checker now requires exactly one 0.05 mm default continuous stroke
with one width and one type. The design files themselves did not change. This
closes a source-geometry gap, not a new manufacturing tolerance or approval.
